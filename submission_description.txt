This Week 1 project presents a data-driven strategic approach to improving logistics performance through data analysis and predictive modeling. The project focuses on a realistic logistics scenario involving shipment delivery between regional hubs using road, rail, and air transportation. The primary business objective is to reduce delivery delays while maintaining control over transportation costs.

The proposed analysis begins with data collection covering shipment distance, weight, transport mode, expected delivery time, actual delivery time, and transport cost. The data will then be cleaned and validated before exploratory analysis is performed. Key performance indicators include On-Time Delivery Rate, Delay Rate, Average Delivery Time, Average Transport Cost, and Cost per Kilometer. These KPIs provide management with a measurable view of service quality and operational efficiency.

The project also demonstrates how data-science methods such as exploratory data analysis, predictive classification, regression, clustering, and optimization can be applied to logistics problems. A logistic-regression baseline is proposed to identify shipments with higher delay risk. The resulting insights can support proactive intervention, transport-mode comparison, route review, and better resource allocation.

The strategic roadmap is structured as Data Collection → Data Cleaning → Exploratory Data Analysis → KPI Analysis → Predictive Modeling → Business Decision. The GitHub project includes a reproducible synthetic dataset, Python implementation, requirements file, README, and the complete Week 1 report. For future production implementation, public freight-flow data such as the U.S. Department of Transportation Freight Analysis Framework can be incorporated.
